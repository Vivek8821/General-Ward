Section 12 — Patient Discharge Instructions
  1. REST: Take complete rest for 7–10 days. No physical exertion, exercise, or strenuous activity until cleared by your doctor at Day 7 review.
  2. HYDRATION: Drink minimum 3–4 litres of fluid daily — water, coconut water, ORS, or fresh juice. You may feel thirsty or tired — this is normal during dengue recovery.
  3. DIET: Eat light, easily digestible, high-nutrition meals. Diabetic diet (low sugar, low oil). Papaya and papaya leaf extract encouraged. Avoid spicy, oily, and fried food for 2 weeks.
  4. BLOOD SUGAR: Monitor fasting and post-meal blood sugar daily. Keep a written log. Target: Fasting <110 mg/dL. Continue Metformin as prescribed — do not stop.
  5. BLOOD PRESSURE: Monitor BP every morning. Target: <130/80 mmHg. Continue Amlodipine daily. If BP >160/100 or severe headache, go to emergency.
  6. MEDICINES: Take ALL prescribed medicines as directed. Do NOT take Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, or Naproxen — these worsen bleeding risk and damage the stomach during dengue recovery. Paracetamol ONLY for fever/pain.
  7. ALCOHOL: Complete abstinence from alcohol for minimum 4 weeks. Your liver enzymes are elevated — alcohol will cause additional liver damage during recovery.
  8. MOSQUITO PREVENTION: Use mosquito repellent (DEET-based) on exposed skin. Sleep under a mosquito net. Wear full-sleeved clothing. Remove all standing water from around your home.

⚠ DENGUE WARNING SIGNS — GO TO EMERGENCY IMMEDIATELY

Bleeding from gums, nose, skin (petechiae), urine (pink/red), or stool (black/tarry). Severe abdominal pain. Persistent vomiting. Platelet count below 50,000 on any home report. Sudden fever returning after 2–3 days of being afebrile. Difficulty breathing. Severe fatigue or fainting. Cold/clammy skin.
